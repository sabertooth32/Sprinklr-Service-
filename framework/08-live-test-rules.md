# 08 · Live-test rules — see the brand as its own customer does

**Goal:** understand the experience the way an end user lives it. Not as a vendor. Not as an auditor.

## Who does what

| Who | Does | Does not |
|---|---|---|
| Agents | Read public pages, reviews, news | Contact the brand in any way |
| The SDR | Runs 5–8 short live tests as an ordinary member of the public | Mention Sprinklr, sales, research or an audit |

## Your persona for live tests

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Who you are | An ordinary member of the public. A possible or existing customer with an everyday question |
| Name | Normally none is needed. If asked, give a first name only. The default is **"Alex"** |
| Employer | Never mentioned. Never say Sprinklr. Never say you work in customer experience or technology |
| Email and phone | Personal ones only. Never a Sprinklr address, signature, device name or caller ID |
| Social accounts | A personal profile with no employer shown, or do not test social at all. Read public replies instead |
| Tone | Polite, brief, natural. Say thank you. End the chat properly |

## Stay honest: the lines you never cross

| Never | Why |
|---|---|
| Claim an account, card, transaction or loss you do not have | A false report to a bank or telco can trigger real fraud or security processes |
| File a complaint about something that did not happen | It wastes their time and creates a false record |
| Give false details to pass an identity check | That is deception with legal risk. Stop before any identity step |
| Use another person's identity, real or invented in full | First name only, nothing more |
| Fill in a lead, application or complaint form | It creates a record and a follow-up for their staff |
| Record a call, or note a staff member's name | Recording and privacy laws differ by country |
| Run many tests or repeat them the same day | Keep it light. One contact per channel is enough |

Ask **general** questions. "What should I do if I see a transaction I do not recognise?" is honest. "I have a fraudulent transaction on my card" is not, unless it is true.

If you are a real customer of the brand, test with your own real, everyday needs. That is the most authentic view.

## If they ask who you are or why you ask

| They ask | You say |
|---|---|
| "Are you a customer?" | The truth: yes, or "not yet, I am looking into it" |
| "Can I have your account or ID number?" | "No need, it is a general question. Thank you." Then end politely |
| "Why are you asking?" | "I want to understand how this works before I need it." |

Never lie about being from a company if asked directly. You are asking as an individual, so simply stay an individual and close the chat.

## What happens to what you learn

| Do | Do not |
|---|---|
| Write your own notes: time to reply, steps, hand-offs, outcome | Share screenshots or transcripts with the prospect |
| Use it to choose which break point to lead with | Quote or name any staff member |
| Describe it later as "what a customer sees" | Say "we tested", "we audited" or "we mystery-shopped you" |

Outreach is different. When you email or call a prospect, you are openly an SDR from Sprinklr. The infographic is built from public sources and a customer's-eye view. It is respectful and names strengths first.
